Inkscript

A scanned Arabic page that selects and copies like typed text. Every picture here is a real output.

    Storyboard · 2026-09-18

    The page's own ink becomes its font

    Scanned journals are pictures. OCR layers put invisible typed text over them, and copying from that text is a lottery in Arabic. Inkscript traces every printed word's ink into a glyph of its own, tells the viewer what each glyph says, and checks the result the way a reader would: by what Chrome copies out.

    99.9%of 84,186 lines come back in reading order
    0pixels substituted: every glyph is the printed occurrence's own outline

    02 · THE PIPELINE

    What a word says, and what a mark is

    Two independent readings of the same scan meet in the PDF. OCR says what each word says; geometry says what each mark is and exactly where. Neither is allowed to edit the other.

    OCR half · inkscript.ocr

    Text and boxes

    • Azure prebuilt-read: every word with its polygon, every line in reading order.
    • Gemini re-reads page 1 only — the title and author lines Azure mangles — with a strict transcription prompt, then a masthead crop, then a title-only extraction if the recitation filter refuses.
    • Gemini's tokens are aligned into Azure's boxes (runs of five words anchor, gaps fill).
    meet in⤵inkscript.pdf
    Geometry half · inkscript.geometry

    Ink and outlines

    • Binarise at 300 dpi (Otsu); rules and table borders cut out first.
    • Connected components → outline polygons (holes filled even-odd), integer pixel coordinates.
    • Blobs are assigned to word boxes; where the ink splits the way the script dictates, a word becomes pieces.
    • A document alphabet labels every shape — knowledge, never a substitute drawing.

    04 · HOW CHROME READS A LINE

    Stored backwards on purpose

    A PDF viewer does not know Arabic; it rebuilds a line from glyph positions and a bidi pass of its own. Chrome's engine (pdfium) reverses the order of a line's segments and each Arabic segment in place, mirroring brackets, while digits, vowel marks and Latin keep their order. So the text is stored as the exact inverse of that — and Chrome gives the line back. Type a line to see all three forms.

    The first storage rule was tuned against pypdfium2 5.13, whose pdfium build 7999 has this automatic direction switched off. It passed the words-intact check — which ignores order — while Chrome itself read vowelled words backwards. Reading pdfium's source for Chrome's branch settled it; the verifier is now pinned to build 7947 and reports lines in order. Try a vowelled word above under the 7999 reading.

    07 · THE ALPHABET AS WITNESS

    Same ink, different text: one of them is wrong

    Every shape in a document is labelled; a word's ink signature is the sequence of its shape ids. When two words share a signature but not a text, the OCR contradicted itself, and the crops make the call easy. Numbers get the same treatment from a second, independent reading.

    Three sets, one reader, final build

    09 · WHAT'S NEXT

    Open, in the order I'd take them

    1. Typeset PDFs whose fonts have no usable encoding: give them a correct ToUnicode from the OCR words — the born-digital variant of this project.
    2. Table pages: cells pdfium joins across rows; the OCR's line order there.
    3. Letter-level selection inside a connected run — the remaining selection limit.
    4. Firefox: pdf.js splits lines at wide gaps and merges tight ones; measured, decision deferred (experiments/06).
    5. A larger unattended sample, read the same way.
