Inkscript

A scanned Arabic page that selects and copies like typed text. Every picture here is a real output.

    Storyboard · 2026-09-20

    The page's own ink becomes its font

    Scanned journals are pictures. OCR layers put invisible typed text over them, and copying from that text is a lottery in Arabic. Inkscript traces every printed word's ink into a glyph of its own, cuts joined words into their letters by following the ink like a pen, tells the viewer what each glyph says, and checks the result the way a reader would: by what Chrome copies out.

    99.99%of 893,203 words copy out intact across 227 journals, 4,440 pages (Chrome's engine, 2026-09-20)
    99.95%of 82,613 lines come back in reading order
    96.8%of words have a selection box for every letter — on 194 scanned documents; a day earlier, none

    02 · THE PIPELINE

    What a word says, and what a mark is

    Two independent readings of the same scan meet in the PDF. OCR says what each word says; geometry says what each mark is and exactly where. Neither is allowed to edit the other.

    OCR half · inkscript.ocr

    Text and boxes

    • Azure prebuilt-read: every word with its polygon, every line in reading order.
    • Gemini re-reads page 1 only — the title and author lines Azure mangles — with a strict transcription prompt, then a masthead crop, then a title-only extraction if the recitation filter refuses.
    • Gemini's tokens are aligned into Azure's boxes (runs of five words anchor, gaps fill).

    Geometry half · inkscript.geometry

    Ink and outlines

    • Binarise at 300 dpi (Otsu); rules and table borders cut out first.
    • Connected components → outline polygons (holes filled even-odd), integer pixel coordinates.
    • Blobs are assigned to word boxes; where the ink splits the way the script dictates, a word becomes pieces.
    • A document alphabet labels every shape — knowledge, never a substitute drawing.

    THE LETTERS

    A joined word is one blob of ink. Where does a letter end?

    A website never has this problem: it places one glyph per letter and knows every box because it put it there. A scan is the reverse — finished ink, the boxes gone. Left uncut, a viewer slices the word into equal parts, which is wrong as soon as letters differ in width. So the ink is followed the way a pen wrote it, and a cut becomes a point on that path, not a line down the page.

    1 / 8

    measured

    Cutting at columns of the page was tried first. It put 90.9% of cuts on the join; doing nothing — the viewer's equal slices — puts 91.6% there. Every method since is compared with that free baseline. The pen path also keeps a kaf's arm with its kaf when the arm hangs over the next letter, which no vertical cut can do.

    THE ATLAS

    No typeface is configured. Each book teaches its own.

    Which of the possible cut points are the right ones? Two witnesses decide. Hard facts that hold in any typeface — a letter's dots, tall stroke and bowl must lie in its own stretch of the path. And the book itself: every cut-out letter goes on a pile with the others of its form, the average of the pile is the book's picture of that letter, the cuts are re-chosen to match the pictures, the pictures rebuilt — four rounds. Drag the slider and watch a 1970s typeface come into focus out of its own pages.

    Witness one: facts

    ف carries one dot above; ي two below; ل stands tall; final ن drops a bowl below the line; no letter is a bare connecting stroke. A fact is enforced for a letter-form only if 85% of its printings in this book show it.

    Witness two: the book

    The pictures above. Alone they let a big letter swallow a small neighbour; the facts alone cannot tell two plain teeth apart. Together: by eye about 50 of 60 random pieces cut right, where half were before.

    A FAILURE WORTH KEEPING

    395 copies of one word, all wrong, all agreeing

    One journal prints في with the ya's tail running back to the right under the fa. The pen path started at the rightmost ink on the baseline — the tail's tip — so every copy had its two letters swapped. The atlas did not object: all 395 copies agreed with each other. It was found by building that book's font and seeing a blank where ف should be. Now, when a fact fails (the fa's dot was inside the ya), the path is tried again from the first letter's body, and the start the facts prefer is kept.

    the lesson

    A witness learned from the data can confirm its own mistake. Something from outside it has to be able to overrule — here the facts. And the letter-coverage number could not see this at all: every letter had a box; the boxes were on the wrong letters.

    04 · HOW CHROME READS A LINE

    Stored backwards on purpose

    A PDF viewer does not know Arabic; it rebuilds a line from glyph positions and a bidi pass of its own. Chrome's engine (pdfium) reverses the order of a line's segments and each Arabic segment in place, mirroring brackets, while digits, vowel marks and Latin keep their order. So the text is stored as the exact inverse of that — and Chrome gives the line back. Type a line to see all three forms.

    The first storage rule was tuned against pypdfium2 5.13, whose pdfium build 7999 has this automatic direction switched off. It passed the words-intact check — which ignores order — while Chrome itself read vowelled words backwards. Reading pdfium's source for Chrome's branch settled it; the verifier is now pinned to build 7947 and reports lines in order. Try a vowelled word above under the 7999 reading.

    07 · THE ALPHABET AS WITNESS

    Same ink, different text: one of them is wrong

    Every shape in a document is labelled; a word's ink signature is the sequence of its shape ids. When two words share a signature but not a text, the OCR contradicted itself, and the crops make the call easy. Numbers get the same treatment from a second, independent reading.

    THE TYPEFACE

    The letters, cut out, are a font. Type something.

    Every letter-form the book prints becomes a glyph, with the joining rules Arabic needs, and the result installs like any font. The text below is set live in fonts built from two scanned journals — nothing here is an image. The restored version is not any one printing: up to fifteen of the best printings of a letter vote on its body, so a break in one fills in and a blot in one disappears.

    Restoration lives in the font. The faithful PDF still draws every occurrence with its own ink — drawing one with another's changes a median 15% of its pixels. A restored edition of a page would be a third output beside it, never a replacement. Known faults of these fonts: a short document never prints some forms (they borrow a relative), no digits or punctuation, uneven gaps.

    AN HONEST EXPERIMENT

    Reading a page with no OCR at all: 41%

    The atlas was taught on four pages and given the fifth blind — no text, not even how many letters. It read lone letters fairly well and joined words badly, for the oldest reason in the field: you must cut before you can match, and match before you can cut. So it is not a reader. It may yet be a checker: cut by the word the OCR gave, then ask whether the ink fits a look-alike word better.

    WHAT IT DOES NOT DO, AND WHAT IS NEXT

    The text is still the OCR's reading

    "Words intact" means the OCR's reading was preserved, not that it was right. Everything selects beautifully, including the words Azure misread. That is the largest gap between this and a perfect digital copy, and the next milestone.

    Trust the textA second opinion from the book's own letters, as a checker; the review-and-correct tools already exist.
    Measure placement96.8% of words have a box per letter; how many boxes sit exactly right needs a hand-checked sample.
    Vowelled wordsNever cut into letters: Chrome reorders the pieces of a split vowelled word. Up to 6% of some documents.
    Underlines, tables, slanted facesUnderlines glued to ink cut badly; table cells join across rows; an overhanging kaf in a fine face is still weak.
    Other viewersFirefox copies lines word-reversed at our nominal size; the fix costs Chrome 0.1%, so Chrome wins.
    A restored editionThe same page with damaged letters redrawn from the restored font — a third output, never a replacement.
