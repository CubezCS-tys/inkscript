Exam proof pack: the second 300

An independent check of the held-out exam (300 unseen articles, 3,404 pages, frozen pipeline v3.3, Argon, Batch). Every page, citation and article below came from a seeded random draw, not chosen by hand. I looked at each scan myself. No paid model calls. 2026-10-20.

Verdict: the product works on unseen documents, but it is not yet error-free enough to skip a safety net, and the current net misses the worst errors. On 60 random pages, 35 were fully right, 23 had only small slips and 2 had a serious error. Both serious errors are table cells left empty that are printed on the scan. The words themselves are very good: 0 wrong words out of 1,532 checked letter by letter. Reading order, notes, verse and pictures were right on almost every page. The weak spots are the author field in the metadata (honorifics kept in names, extra "authors") and loose or missing citation fields. The bigger issue is that neither serious page was flagged, so the judge never saw them. The judge's automatic edits never made a page worse, but only 9 of 14 were clearly right.

Words, compared line by line

I compared 10 of the 60 pages with the scan word by word (seed 20261021): 1,532 words. No word was wrong. There were 13 spelling-level differences (0.85%, 95% interval 0.5–1.4%). Twelve came from Azure normalising the printed spelling: a bare ان stored as أن (8 times on one page), or a kasra stored as tanween. One came from the model adding a hamza (فاحيت → فأحيت). Printer misprints were kept and marked as sic. The model also repaired many words Azure had misread, for example in fully vowelled poetry (تمشي, وخطوها, تصونهم) and in a blurred banner byline (تص محمدقارويت → شعر: محمد هارون الحلو).

The safety net: flags and the judge

On my 60 pages the code raised flags on 13. I judged 8 of those 13 fully right, so most flags point at pages that are fine. The 2 serious pages and 18 of the 23 minor pages were not flagged, and the judge only sees flagged pages plus 50 random ones. Recall of my serious problems: 0 of 2 by the flags, 0 of 2 by the judge. On my pages the judge produced only 2 findings: a correct advertisement mark (1539 p2) and a note-link proposal sent to the owner.

What still fails (with examples)

  1. Table cells silently dropped. In 0201 p11, 13 of 60 cells are empty (row numbers) although they are printed. In 0295 p3, 3 numbers are missing. Neither page was flagged.
  2. Authors in the metadata: honorifics kept in names, Latin spellings and translators added as authors, rapporteurs listed as authors (see the metadata section).
  3. Hijri years in the year field with no calendar mark (1409, 1428, 1429).
  4. Quote leftovers: a stray ")" before » (الفلسطيني)»). I counted 408 occurrences of ")»" across the 300 masters.
  5. No inline styling: subscripts, superscripts and italics are flattened everywhere.
  6. Superscript and roman note markers (i, ii, xiii in a French article) are glued to the note text and not linked to their calls.
  7. Interlinear linguistic glosses are flattened into running lines (0753).
  8. Calligraphic headline pictures keep no description text (0549 p2: "والنشاط الإسلامي" is not searchable).
  9. Page images: 0529's PDF has no embedded Arabic font, so the page image that Gemini and the views see has blank Arabic text. The output is still right because Azure read the PDF's text layer, but the image check was blind on that article.

…and what works, from the same random draw

Gold set, run-to-run variance, cost and speed

  • Gold check (gold43.py check): all 5 gold articles are unchanged under today's code (950aa361c0). This checks that the code has not regressed on the first sample. It does not measure accuracy on the exam.
  • Two runs of the same pipeline (v3.3 low, 3 hard articles, 38 pages, from step G0): 14,196 words were compared. Words placed in a different kind of block: 1. Words present in only one run: 518 (3.6%), almost all on an advertisement page of 1010-006. 6 of 38 pages have a different number of blocks. Against the gold snapshots, two v3.3 runs differ by 9–98 items per article, and the 94 kind differences on 1140 are the same in both runs. In short, a second run moves words around at the edges, mostly on advertisements, and rarely changes what a block is. These 3 articles were picked because the arms disagreed, so typical pages vary less.
  • Cost (from the spend log): run $27.07 (501 requests), judge $4.34 (599 pages), retries $0.54 (15). Total $31.94 = $0.106 per article (£0.096 incl. 20% VAT) = $0.0094 per page (£0.0085), so about $106k (£96k) per million articles at 11.3 pages each. The builders note the price doubles from 1 Dec 2026.
  • Speed and failures: each Batch job took 2.4–7 min. The run took 2 h 46 min from launch to the last answer, including a 64-minute laptop reboot. 3 of 491 first-round requests failed (2 cut off at the output limit, 1 malformed JSON). They were asked again in smaller chunks and all 10 retry requests succeeded. All 300 masters are valid.
